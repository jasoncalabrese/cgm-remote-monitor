The ask

Review the overnight basal rates (22:30 – 04:30). Loop is currently delivering far less basal than the schedule specifies — ~24.5 U/day actual vs 38.05 U/day scheduled — and suspends 27–38% of the time overnight. The scheduled rate is what the pod falls back to if Loop stops (dead phone, lost pump comms), which is a meaningful concern now that he lives alone at college.

He has had recurrent nocturnal severe lows (nadirs of 40, 43, 50 mg/dL in the past week), all self-treated and resolved. Alarms, fast carbs and a roommate are in place.

A second item worth equal attention: the correction target is a flat 100 mg/dL across all 24 hours, and 81% of Loop's automatic overnight insulin is given below 150 mg/dL. Raising the overnight target may act more directly on the lows than the basal change, and requires no change in behaviour (§4).

6 · Limitations

Sample. Marching band began ~Aug 10 and brought both more activity and early wake times. Days with an early start (first meal before 9am, n=7) ran 4.0% below 54 mg/dL versus 0.9% on late-start days (n=52). However those early days were band camp plus one Saturday event — not the ordinary semester, where first class will be around 9am. Activity-specific conclusions are therefore not yet reliable and should be re-derived after a few weeks of routine data.

What is not schedule-dependent is the gap between scheduled and delivered basal in §1 — it holds on early days (20.4 U/day delivered), late days (24.5 U/day), and was present before the move.

Other. Inhaled insulin doses are not logged and are invisible here. Small carbohydrate entries are sometimes used to prompt Loop to correct, so carb totals overstate food intake. G7 sensor-compression artifacts (brief untreated dips that rebound spontaneously) were excluded from glucose statistics; they are frequent overnight and would otherwise inflate the low percentages.
